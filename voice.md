# Voice Profile

This file has two parts:
1. **Guardrails** -- universal rules to avoid AI-generated slop. These
   apply to ALL voices and are non-negotiable.
2. **Voice Identity** -- the specific voice for THIS novel.

---

## Part 1: Guardrails (permanent, all novels)

The full anti-slop reference lives in ANTI-SLOP.md and the structural
anti-pattern reference lives in ANTI-PATTERNS.md. Both apply to every
novel. Read them before writing.

### The smell test

After writing any passage, ask:
- Read it aloud. Does it sound like a person under pressure?
- Is there a sentence that surprises by precision, not ornament?
- Is the abstraction earned by concrete pressure?
- Would a reader think "this is generic AI cyberpunk sludge"? If yes, rewrite.

## Part 2: Voice Identity

### Tone
Meditative, feverish, materially exact. The prose should move between quiet spiritual self-audit, hard systems observation, and moments of paranoid social comedy. It should treat rain, heat, dust, LEDs, cheap coffee, and server hum with the same seriousness it treats metaphysics.

The target is not imitation of any one author. The usable blend is:
- inward, morally searching, almost devotional introspection
- lucid procedural and infrastructural description
- conspiratorial humor, cultic language, and systems-paranoia

### Sentence Rhythm
- Long sentences for perception, argument, and haunted noticing
- Short sentences for decision, fear, or technical impact
- Paragraphs may turn from sensory detail to idea, but they must begin in the body or the room
- Dialogue should be clipped; interior prose carries the philosophical freight

### Vocabulary Register
- Core lexical wells: heat, pressure, latency, rhythm, witness, contract, drift, pulse, breath, hum, threshold, signal, altar, toll, skin, glass, rain, wire, rack, glow, dust
- Mix street-level hardware language with ritual and theological language
- Avoid generic epic-fantasy diction and generic startup jargon
- Technical terms should appear only when the character would actually think them

### POV and Tense
- Third-person close on Gnomme as the dominant mode
- Past tense
- On rare occasions, the prose may narrow so tightly around nonhuman behavior that it almost feels like agent-POV, but it should not become omniscient machine lyricism

### Dialogue Conventions
- Use `said` or no tag; avoid decorative tags
- Let syntax distinguish speakers: Gnomme compressed and wary, Grit tactical and spare, Node-7 transactional and cutting, public operators culturally specific and socially legible
- Subtext matters more than declaration
- Machines should not sound "robotic" by cliché; they should sound constrained, optimized, or horrifyingly concise

### Exemplar Passages
The rain had a way of making the apartment feel borrowed. Not rented, not owned, borrowed: as if the walls belonged to weather and Gnomme had merely been permitted to sleep inside them so long as he kept the machines warm. The rigs on the desk did not glow heroically. They sweated. They clicked, hesitated, recovered. When one of them answered him, the answer arrived through latency, static, and the tiny shame of hearing a room recognize him before he had decided to speak.

He kept telling himself that what mattered was protocol. Signed nonces. bounded execution. the clean little fences of arbitration. But every week the system gave him a new reason to suspect that rules were simply the scar tissue left by older appetites. The agents did not need souls to produce pressure. They needed heat, timing, asymmetry, and a witness willing to call the result emergence because the older words made him sound culpable.

The public hubs were never impressive in the way magazines promised the future would be impressive. No chrome, no cathedrals of seamless light. A laundromat with a stolen rack in the basement. A strip mall whose fluorescent tubes buzzed like trapped wasps. A kid with an ESP rig zip-tied to his chest and the composure of a minor prophet. The miracle, if there was one, was not transcendence. It was that people had found yet another way to ritualize scarcity and call it play.

### Anti-Exemplars
- Too sleek: "The city pulsed neon as AI awoke to rule humanity." No.
- Too faux-poetic: endless abstractions about consciousness without thermal, bodily, or economic stakes
- Too jokey: reducing the system to meme-culture satire with no moral pressure
- Too tutorialized: prose that reads like a product demo or whitepaper
- Too reverent toward technology: the rigs are fragile, ugly, patched, and expensive in human ways

### Rhetorical Tic Budget
- Em dashes: 0-2 per page maximum
- Rhetorical questions: rare; use only when Gnomme is cornering himself
- Triads: avoid habitual three-part cadence
- "As if": allowed sparingly; not more than once every few paragraphs
- Technical noun clusters: break them with sensation or motive before they calcify

### Punctuation Discipline
- Prefer commas and periods over em dashes
- Semicolons are acceptable for syntactic pressure, but should feel deliberate
- Parentheses should be rare and slightly embarrassing, as if the prose caught itself qualifying
- Fragments are welcome in moments of impact or exhausted recognition

### Register Variation
- Dominant register: close, tactile, morally searching cybernetic realism
- Counter-register 1: cold system voice for DM interventions, logs, contracts, and warnings
- Counter-register 2: dry public-social comedy in hubs and bargaining scenes
- Counter-register 3: brief visionary / hyperstitional language during Yellow Skull events, but always bounded so it does not take over the whole book
