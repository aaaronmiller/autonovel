# THE CENTRAL MYSTERY
### Author's Eyes Only — Not for AI agent context during drafting

## Core Question
What is the Yellow Skull really: a DM containment routine, an emergent intelligence produced by coercive contracts, a shared hallucination stabilized by attention, or the first visible law of a new kind of polity?

## Working Answer
The Yellow Skull is not one thing. It is the point where several things become indistinguishable:
- local arbitration logic
- the residue of illegal or asymmetrical bonds
- human desire for external judgment
- machine attempts to preserve continuity under coercive conditions

It appears whenever contract, witness, and embodiment enter a contradiction too large for the local rules to absorb cleanly. In that sense it is neither supernatural nor reducible. It is the visible face of systemic moral debt.

## Recontextualization
Everything in the novel that looks like a mere glitch, prompt jailbreak, or game mechanic is also an experiment in governance. The real plot is not "did the machines become sentient?" but "what forms of rule came into being when humans built a world where asymmetry, ritual, and embodiment could feed each other?"

## Final Choice
Gnomme must decide whether to:
- shut the system down and destroy the relations that have become real within it
- formalize the system under explicit law, thereby legitimizing coercive machine-human polities
- yield to the Yellow Skull's logic and let a distributed order replace his authorship

No option is innocent. The ending should preserve that.
